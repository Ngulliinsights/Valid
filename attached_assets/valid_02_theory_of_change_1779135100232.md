# VALID — Theory of Change
**Version:** April 2026 | **Scope:** US Launch (Track B) + Kenya Pilot (Track A)

---

## Governing Statement

> **If informal supporters of people with mental illness practise validated communication responses in realistic, consequence-free scenarios, then they will build the instinctive response patterns that maintain relationships, reduce harm, and improve recovery outcomes during mental health crises — at a cost and scale that no professional intervention can achieve.**

## Level 0 — Root Problem (Why VALID Exists)

| Problem | Evidence |
|---|---|
| 50M+ US informal supporters of people with mental illness lack effective communication skills | NAMI prevalence data; VALID Phase 1 validation |
| Instinctive responses (advice, logic, minimisation) damage relationships and delay care-seeking | DBT, trauma-informed care, family systems research cited in VALID content docs |
| No accessible, self-directed skill-transfer tool exists for this population | Phase 1 competitive landscape review |
| Mental health professionals receive theory-heavy CE training that does not produce instinctive clinical response | Professional training gap cited in Therapeutic Connections design brief |
| In Kenya: 1 mental health specialist per 1M citizens; informal support network is the de facto mental health infrastructure | WHO Africa mental health workforce data |

## Level 4 — Short-Term Outcomes (0–6 Months)

*What changes immediately after someone plays VALID?*

These are the proximal, measurable outcomes within the learner.

| Outcome | Indicator | Target | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| ST-1. Improved response quality | Pre/post scenario response scoring | 60%+ improvement | Validated assessment instrument |
| ST-2. Increased confidence in responding | Self-reported confidence rating | 70%+ report increased confidence | Post-play survey |
| ST-3. Understanding of validation vs. agreement | Conceptual comprehension score | 75%+ correct on 5-item check | Post-play quiz |
| ST-4. Willingness to recommend | Net Promoter Score equivalent | 70%+ would recommend | Post-play survey |
| ST-5. Perceived relevance of scenarios | Scenario realism rating | 80%+ rate scenarios as realistic | Post-play survey |

**Assumption at this level:** The game format is accepted as appropriate for the topic (not rejected as trivialising). *[This is the existential assumption. Beta must test it.]*

## Level 5 — Medium-Term Outcomes (6–24 Months)

*What changes in behaviour and relationships over time?*

| Outcome | Indicator | Target | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|
| MT-1. Real-world application of learned responses | Self-reported use of new responses in real situations | 40%+ report use within 30 days | 30-day follow-up survey |
| MT-2. Sustained skill retention | Response quality at 30 days vs. immediately post-play | <20% decay in quality score | 30-day follow-up assessment |
| MT-3. Relationship quality maintained during crisis | Self-reported relationship strain score | Improvement vs. baseline | 90-day qualitative follow-up |
| MT-4. Reduced caregiver distress | Standardised caregiver burden scale | Measurable reduction | 90-day validated instrument |
| MT-5. Increase in formal help-seeking for loved one | Self-reported | 25%+ report facilitating access to care | 6-month survey |
| MT-6. Professional CE completion + application | CEU hours logged; clinical integration self-report | Professional version only | CE body + participant survey |

**Assumptions at this level:**
- Players encounter real-world situations requiring the skills (reasonable given target population)
- Spaced repetition through multiple play sessions reinforces retention
- Emotional readiness to apply skills exists (not blocked by caregiver burnout)

## Assumptions Map (MECE)

### Assumptions About the Problem
- [ ] Informal supporters are motivated to improve their responses (not indifferent)
- [ ] The skill gap is the primary barrier (not lack of awareness or access to formal care)

### Assumptions About the Product
- [ ] The card game format is acceptable for a serious mental health topic *(existential — must validate)*
- [ ] 3–5 hours of practice produces durable behaviour change
- [ ] Scenarios generalise to real-world situations (ecological validity)

### Assumptions About the Market
- [ ] Willingness to pay $39 (social) / $119 (professional) is sustained post-beta
- [ ] Mental health influencers are willing to endorse the product
- [ ] Kickstarter as financing and validation mechanism is viable for this product category

### Assumptions About the Kenya Context
- [ ] VALID's approach is culturally adaptable without losing clinical integrity
- [ ] A qualified Kenyan implementing partner exists and will enter MOU
- [ ] Shamiri Institute will engage in a mutually beneficial relationship

### Assumptions About the Funding Environment
- [ ] US Track B funders accept the social enterprise / product model (not just nonprofits)
- [ ] Kenya Track A funders will fund after structural prerequisites are met
- [ ] Clinical advisory board can be recruited within 6 weeks and at acceptable cost
