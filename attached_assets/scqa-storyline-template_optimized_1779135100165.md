## Step 0 — Choose Your Opening Mode

Before writing your SCQA, decide how this presentation opens. The opening mode is not decoration — it is the frame through which your audience receives everything that follows.

| Mode | Best For | First Words Feel Like |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** (Poetic/Rhythmic) | Opening a room, keynote, high-stakes pitch | A contrast that stops you cold |
| **Plain Pitch** (Clarity) | Investors, institutions, decision-makers | Architecture without flourish |
| **Problem/Solution** (Persuasion) | One-on-one conversations, moving someone to action | A gap and the only bridge across it |
| **Emotional Close** | Sealing commitment, closing a session | An outcome you can feel |

Most presentations benefit from one dominant mode with deliberate gear-shifts. Write your SCQA first. Assign your mode second. They should reinforce each other.

## Communication Mode Reference Card

**Hook (Poetic/Rhythmic)**
Technique: Rhythm + contrast. Make the opening unexpected.
Hallmark: The audience doesn't see the pivot coming.
Example opening: "Mental health is everywhere — on our timelines, on our T-shirts. But awareness without understanding is just noise with good intentions."

**Plain Pitch (Clarity Layer)**
Technique: Architecture over flourish. State the structure first.
Hallmark: A decision-maker could act on this in 60 seconds.
Example opening: "Valid is a card game that builds genuine literacy around mental illness — not to diagnose, but to respond."

**Problem/Solution Frame (Persuasion Structure)**
Technique: Name the gap before offering the bridge.
Hallmark: The audience feels the problem before hearing the solution.
Example opening: "Here's the gap. Awareness created a generation that knows the vocabulary without the fluency."

**Emotional Close (Outcome Anchor)**
Technique: Name the transformation, not the mechanism.
Hallmark: The audience remembers the feeling, not the features.
Example opening: "Think about the last time someone in your life was struggling — and you didn't know what to say. This is built for that moment."
