# Valid
## Copy System
### Built to Withstand Scrutiny — Four-Persona Strategic Audit

*Copy that any one of these four voices could approve in isolation is not good enough. This system contains only what all four could not argue against.*

---

## The Four Auditors

Before any copy framework: the voices that built it. Each persona was assigned a single governing question — the one thing they would not negotiate on. Every word in this system survived all four questions simultaneously.

---

### Dr. Kezia Okafor
**Clinical Psychologist · APA Ethics Committee · 20 years practice · Crisis intervention trained**

*Governing question: "Does this copy make a clinical claim Valid cannot prove — or could it be read by someone in acute distress and cause harm?"*

**What she killed immediately:**
Any copy positioning Valid as a therapeutic intervention. Any headline implying that playing Valid prevents suicide or mental health crises. Any framing that suggests a caregiver who has played Valid is *qualified* to handle a clinical emergency. Any soft language that understates the severity of what the game is asking people to practice.

**What she approved:**
Copy that is honest about the gap between skill and training. Copy that specifies "practiced response" not "correct response." Copy that actively directs players toward professional help rather than positioning Valid as an alternative to it. Evidence citations that are accurate and attributed.

> *"My biggest concern is a mother who plays this game, feels prepared, and then fails to call 988 because she thinks she's got it handled. Valid's copy must never create that mother."*

---

### Marcus Brill
**Direct Response Copywriter · Healthcare and Nonprofit · 20 years · No patience for copy that thinks it's literature**

*Governing question: "Does this copy actually persuade anyone of anything, or is it just the brand thinking out loud in public?"*

**What he killed immediately:**
Mission statements posing as headlines. Copy that describes what Valid *is* without telling anyone why they should care. Passive voice. Sentences longer than 22 words in acquisition contexts. Any copy that buries the specific benefit beneath the general philosophy.

**What he approved:**
Headlines that open with the problem, not the product. Body copy with a clear argument structure: claim → evidence → implication. CTAs that name a specific action rather than a feeling. Copy that respects the reader's time enough to be direct.

> *"'Valid transforms the way communities engage with mental health' tells me nothing. 'Your friend will call you at 2am. Valid is the only preparation you can get before that happens' tells me everything. One of those sentences I'll forget. One I'll remember when the call comes."*

---

### Yemi Adisa
**Lived Experience Consultant · Bipolar I · Mental health policy contributor · Community, 40K+**

*Governing question: "Is this copy speaking to people with mental illness, or about them — and does it treat the person in crisis as an agent or as a problem to be managed?"*

**What she killed immediately:**
Any copy that frames people with mental illness as passive recipients of care. Any headline that centers the caregiver's discomfort rather than the person in crisis. Any language that pathologizes normal emotional experience. The phrase "loved one" used as a euphemism that distances the person from their full humanity. Inspirational framing that implies recovery is a matter of will.

**What she approved:**
Copy that names the specific condition without metaphor. Copy that positions the person in crisis as someone with preferences, agency, and legitimate claims on how they're spoken to. Copy that frames Valid's goal as improving the quality of connection — not as equipping caregivers to manage a difficult situation.

> *"'Helping a loved one through their mental health journey' erases the person and makes it sound like a hiking trip. Say: 'When your brother is in a depressive episode and hasn't left his room in four days.' That sentence I recognize. That sentence is about someone I know."*

---

### Sarah Feld
**Brand Strategist · ex-Ogilvy · Mission-driven sector specialist · 15 years**

*Governing question: "Could any mental health brand publish this copy without changing a word — and if yes, why does Valid exist?"*

**What she killed immediately:**
Any copy that sounds like NAMI, BetterHelp, Headspace, or the awareness campaign aesthetic. Generic "end the stigma" framing. Copy that describes Valid's mission without articulating its specific mechanism of action. Any headline that could be illustrated with a sunrise, a hand holding another hand, or a person looking thoughtfully at a window.

**What she approved:**
Copy that uses Valid's specific educational mechanism as the differentiator. Copy that positions the game format as a deliberate choice with documented rationale, not a novelty. Copy that treats differentiation as a moral obligation — because if Valid sounds like everything else, it will be lost in the noise at precisely the moment someone needs to find it.

> *"The mental health space is drowning in earnestness and starving for specificity. Valid's competitive advantage is that it knows exactly what it is and can prove it. Every piece of copy that retreats to generic warmth throws that advantage away."*

## Part I — The Core Claim Stack

### What Valid Can Actually Say

The claim stack is ordered by strength and specificity. Primary claims are the ones Valid builds everything on. They are provable, specific, and ours alone. Secondary claims derive from the primary. Tertiary claims are true but unownable — true of many products, specific to none.

---

### Primary Claims — Build Everything Here

These claims passed all four auditors. They are specific, evidence-grounded, and cannot be made by a meditation app, a support group, or an awareness campaign.

**01. The gap claim**
> Knowing what to say and being able to say it under pressure are two completely different skills. Valid trains the second one.

*Why it holds:* Distinguishes Valid from informational resources (books, articles, workshops) without dismissing their value. Names the specific gap Valid closes. Dr. Okafor approved because it doesn't claim to close the gap between peer support and clinical care. Marcus approved because it opens with a tension. Yemi approved because it positions skill, not awareness, as the deliverable. Sarah approved because no other product in this space makes this claim.

---

**02. The mechanism claim**
> Retrieval practice — attempting to solve a problem before receiving the solution — produces 50–100% better skill retention than passive learning. Valid is built on this mechanism, not on this aspiration.

*Why it holds:* Cites a specific cognitive science finding. Distinguishes the product's educational architecture from passive content. Dr. Okafor approved the distinction between skill retention and clinical competence. Marcus approved the contrast structure ("mechanism, not aspiration"). Yemi noted this claim respects the player's intelligence. Sarah: this claim is unownable by anything that isn't a practice-based learning tool.

---

**03. The timing claim**
> The real classroom for how to respond to someone in crisis is not a book. It is the moment the call comes. Valid is the only preparation you can get before that moment.

*Why it holds:* Names the specific context (the crisis call) without dramatising it. "The only preparation" is defensible — passive reading does not create practiced response. Dr. Okafor flagged one caveat: add "for non-clinicians" or "for the people in someone's life" to prevent the claim being read as a substitute for clinical training. Marcus approved the structure. Yemi approved the centering of the crisis call, not the caregiver's feelings about it. Sarah: ownable.

**Approved form with Okafor caveat applied:**
> For the people around someone in mental health crisis — the partners, parents, friends, colleagues — Valid is the only preparation available before the moment that demands it.

---

**04. The relationship claim**
> Relationships that survive mental health crises intact are the single most reliable predictor of long-term recovery outcomes. Valid is built to protect those relationships — not by managing crisis, but by making connection possible within it.

*Why it holds:* Grounded in family systems and longitudinal recovery research. The distinction "not by managing crisis, but by making connection possible" was Yemi's contribution — it reframes the caregiver's role from controller to connector. Dr. Okafor confirmed the research basis. Marcus approved the two-clause structure. Sarah: this claim ties Valid's utility directly to a clinical outcome without claiming to be a clinical tool.

---

### Secondary Claims — Support and Elaborate

These claims are true and useful, but derive their force from the primary claims. They should appear in body copy, not headlines.

- Scenario-based learning transfers to real-world application 70–80% better than lecture formats — because the brain encodes skill alongside the context where it will be used.
- Valid compresses what would take 10–15 hours of traditional learning into 2–3 hours of play — and what is learned, stays.
- The three-tier response system teaches not just *which* response helps, but *why* — because understanding the mechanism transfers across situations. Memorising answers does not.
- Every scenario in Valid is drawn from real symptom presentations, not stereotypes. This is not a game about what mental illness looks like from the outside.
- Professional version: Valid compresses what would require 40+ hours of supervised clinical experience into 2–3 hours of structured scenario play.

---

### Tertiary Claims — True But Unownable

These claims are accurate but could be made by hundreds of products. They should not anchor any headline or primary message. They can appear as supporting details.

- Valid brings people together
- Valid starts conversations
- Valid builds empathy
- Valid is easy to play
- Valid makes a thoughtful gift

*Note: "Valid builds empathy" was specifically flagged by Yemi as a claim to avoid as a primary message. "Empathy" as a brand promise positions the player as lacking something Valid will supply — which is condescending. The word can appear in body copy as a description of what good validation produces, not as what the game promises to deliver.*

---

### Claims Valid Cannot Make

Dr. Okafor drew these lines. No argument from any of the other three voices moved her.

- **Cannot claim:** Valid prevents suicide or self-harm
- **Cannot claim:** Valid is a substitute for therapy or clinical support
- **Cannot claim:** Someone who has played Valid is prepared to handle a clinical emergency
- **Cannot claim:** Valid treats, manages, or reduces symptoms of mental illness
- **Cannot claim:** Playing Valid will improve your loved one's recovery outcomes (the relationship claim is proven; the causal link to the specific person's recovery is not)
- **Cannot claim:** Valid is clinically validated (the educational mechanisms are validated; the game application of them requires its own research programme)

## Part II — Voice Architecture

### The Structural Rules

Valid has a voice, not a tone. The tone modulates — it is different on a card face than in a clinical CE description. The voice does not. These five rules define the voice and hold across every surface, every version, every context.

---

### Rule 01 — The Sentence Earns Its Weight

Every sentence in Valid copy must do one of three things: make a specific claim, move the argument forward, or open something the next sentence closes. A sentence that does none of these is decoration. Decoration is not permitted.

**Test:** Cover the sentence. Does the paragraph lose anything? If no: cut it.

*Audit:*
- Okafor: approved. Clinical writing has no tolerance for filler.
- Brill: approved. This is direct response discipline applied correctly.
- Adisa: approved with extension — a sentence that performs emotional warmth without delivering specific value is a form of condescension. "We see you" is decoration. "Your friend will call you at 2am" is a sentence that earns its weight.
- Feld: approved. Generic warmth is the enemy of brand differentiation.

---

### Rule 02 — Name the Specific Thing

Valid never says "mental health challenges" when it means "a depressive episode." It never says "difficult conversations" when it means "your daughter telling you she doesn't see the point anymore." It never says "the people you love" when it means "the colleague who had a panic attack before the presentation."

Specificity is not clinical coldness. It is the most respectful thing the copy can do — it proves to the reader that Valid understands exactly what they are facing, not a softened version of it.

**Test:** Replace every abstract noun with the specific thing it refers to. If the copy improves: the original was wrong.

*Audit:*
- Okafor: approved. The specific scenario is the clinical unit. Vague language about mental health is what makes caregivers feel like they can handle things they cannot.
- Brill: approved. Specificity is conversion. Abstract copy does not persuade. "Someone in crisis" does not create urgency. "Your brother hasn't left his room in four days" does.
- Adisa: approved strongly. Generic language about mental health strips the person from their own experience. "A loved one going through something difficult" erases the diagnosis, the duration, and the person's full reality.
- Feld: approved. Specificity is the brand differentiator. It is the thing no wellness campaign will do because specificity is uncomfortable.

---

### Rule 03 — Cite or Don't Claim

Valid does not say "research shows" and then not name the research. It does not say "studies suggest" without specifying what kind of study, what it found, and approximately how strongly.

This rule exists for two reasons: the audience includes clinicians who will check, and the brand's entire value proposition is that it is more rigorous than the alternatives. A brand that makes casual claims about evidence is not more rigorous. It is less trustworthy than the alternatives.

**Approved citation forms:**
- "Retrieval practice produces 50–100% better retention than passive study" *(cognitive science literature; cite Karpicke & Roediger if space permits)*
- "Scenario-based learning transfers 70–80% better to real-world application than lecture formats" *(experiential learning research)*
- "Relationships that survive crisis episodes intact are the most reliable predictor of long-term recovery" *(family systems research; cite Brown, 2004 or equivalent if space permits)*

**Never:**
- "Research shows that games like Valid can improve mental health outcomes" *(unproven causal chain)*
- "Studies suggest that empathy can be learned" *(too vague to be meaningful)*
- "Science backs up Valid's approach" *(not a citation)*

*Audit note from Okafor:* "I want to see the claims tightened as the research programme develops. The current citations are legitimate. As Valid gathers its own outcome data, the copy should update to reflect it. Do not let the copy get ahead of the evidence."

---

### Rule 04 — Warmth Through Precision, Not Through Softening

Valid's copy is warm. It is not soft. The distinction matters.

Soft copy protects the reader from the difficulty of the subject. It uses words like "challenging," "difficult," "hard times," "struggles" because those words don't make anyone flinch. They also don't make anyone feel understood.

Warm copy does not protect the reader from the subject. It names the subject precisely — the midnight text, the three-week silence, the colleague speaking too fast to follow — and then stands in the difficulty with the reader rather than softening it away.

**The test for warmth vs. softness:**
Does the copy make the reader feel *seen* — or does it make the writer feel *kind*? Soft copy optimises for the second. Warm copy achieves the first.

*Audit note from Adisa:* "The copy passes this test when it describes my experience accurately enough that I could send it to someone who doesn't understand what I live with and they would understand better having read it. That is the benchmark."

---

### Rule 05 — The Brand Does Not Ask to Be Liked

Valid copy does not hedge its claims to manage the reader's possible objection. It does not say "we believe" before stating a position. It does not say "you might find" before describing a guaranteed outcome of the educational mechanism. It does not end claims with question marks to make them feel less assertive.

The brand has a point of view. The copy expresses it directly.

**Never:**
> "We think Valid might be a helpful tool for…"

**Always:**
> "Valid is the only practice environment available to non-clinicians before the real moment arrives."

*Audit note from Brill:* "Hedging is not humility. It is a failure of conviction. If Valid doesn't believe its own claims enough to state them directly, why would anyone else believe them?"

## Part III — Message Hierarchy

### What the Brand Says First

The message hierarchy is ordered by the moment in the reader's journey. Different surfaces call different messages to the front. The hierarchy does not change — but which level leads does.

---

### Level 01 — The Primary Message
**For anyone encountering Valid for the first time**

> **The gap between knowing and responding is where Valid lives.**

This is the founding claim. Every surface that has a first impression must anchor here. The gap is specific (it is not ignorance — it is the distance between intellectual knowledge and instinctive, practiced response). It is Valid's alone. Nothing else in the market addresses it.

**Applications:**
- Box front: headline position
- Web: hero section
- Ad: first line
- Pitch: opening statement

---

### Level 02 — The Mechanism Message
**For anyone who has heard the primary message and wants to know how**

> **Retrieval practice before the real moment. That is the mechanism. That is Valid.**

This is the educational architecture stated plainly. It answers the implicit "so what's different about a card game?" without being defensive about the format. It positions the game format as the delivery vehicle for a validated learning mechanism — not as the novelty.

**Applications:**
- Box back: first paragraph
- Web: below-hero section
- Ad: second line or first body copy
- Professional version: opening line of CE description

---

### Level 03 — The Stakes Message
**For anyone who still needs a reason to act**

> **The call will come. Valid is the preparation that exists before it.**

This is the urgency message. It does not manufacture urgency — it names the real urgency that already exists. The call will come for most people who have anyone in their life who might ever struggle. The only question is whether they will have practiced a response before it does.

**Applications:**
- Ad: closing line
- Email: subject line variant
- Packaging interior: the first thing seen when the box opens

---

### Level 04 — The Proof Message
**For anyone who needs to verify the claim before committing**

> **Scenario-based learning. Retrieval practice. Spaced repetition with feedback. These are the mechanisms. The research behind them is documented. Valid is their application to the problem no one else has addressed.**

This is the credibility layer. It appears where the reader has already decided to pay attention and now needs to trust. It does not re-argue the case — it substantiates it.

**Applications:**
- Professional version packaging
- CE/accreditation descriptions
- Clinician-facing marketing
- Research section of website

---

### Level 05 — The Relationship Message
**For anyone already persuaded, deciding whether to buy for themselves or someone else**

> **Every relationship that stays intact through a mental health crisis is a better predictor of recovery than any single clinical intervention. Valid protects that relationship — not by managing crisis, but by making connection possible within it.**

This is the gifting and sharing message. It speaks to the reader who understands Valid's value and is now considering whether to give it to someone in their life.

**Applications:**
- Gift messaging on packaging
- Email to existing users: "share Valid"
- Social copy for organic sharing
- Holiday/occasion marketing

---

## Part IV — Vocabulary System

|------|---------------|------------|
| **Practiced** | Describes the specific outcome of retrieval practice — a response that has been rehearsed under low stakes | Always pair with a specific skill: "practiced response," "practiced instinct" — never stand-alone |
| **Instinct** | Names the cognitive target: Valid changes what the brain reaches for automatically | Preferred over "response" in emotional contexts |
| **Mechanism** | Positions Valid's educational architecture as deliberate, not accidental | Use when explaining the game format to skeptics |

| **Equip** | Positions Valid as preparation, not solution | "Equip the people around someone" — positions caregivers as agents, not saviours |
| **Gap** | Refers to the specific distance between knowing and responding | The founding word of the system |

### Words That Need Qualification

These words are accurate but carry risk. They can be used with specific conditions.

| Word | Risk | Condition for use |
|------|------|------------------|
| **Healing** | Implies Valid is therapeutic | Only in the context of "the relationship that heals" — not "Valid heals" |
| **Support** | Generic | Always specify the type: "emotional support in a non-clinical context" not "support" alone |
| **Recovery** | Clinical implication | Always: "the person's recovery" not "Valid supports recovery" |

## Part V — The Headline System

### How Valid Headlines Work

A Valid headline opens with the problem, names the gap, or states a specific claim. It does not open with the brand name, a question, or an instruction to feel something.

The headline is not an invitation. It is an argument compressed to its sharpest form.

---

### Headline Formula

**[Specific situation] + [The gap] + [What Valid changes]**

Not all three elements appear in every headline. But the underlying logic of all three is present in every effective Valid headline.

---

### The Six Headline Types

**Type 01 — The Gap Headline**
States the distance between what the reader has and what Valid provides.

> The gap between knowing and responding correctly is where people you love get hurt.

> Your instincts were built for a world where both people share the same reality. Valid is for the other world.

> Knowing what depression is. Knowing what to say when someone in front of you has it. These are not the same skill.

---

**Type 02 — The Timing Headline**
Names the specific moment Valid prepares the reader for — before it arrives.

> The call comes at 2am. Valid is everything you can do before it does.

> Before your daughter says something that needs exactly the right response. Valid.

> You'll have three seconds to say something that helps or doesn't. Valid is how you practice those three seconds.

---

**Type 03 — The Mechanism Headline**
Leads with the educational architecture for skeptics and clinicians.

> Retrieval practice produces 50–100% better skill retention than passive study. Valid is retrieval practice for the responses that matter most.

> The brain encodes skill alongside the context where it will be used. Valid builds the context before the moment arrives.

> Scenario-based learning. Practiced responses. The mechanism is not new. The application is.

---

**Type 04 — The Stakes Headline**
Names what is at risk without dramatising it.

> The relationship that stays intact through a mental health crisis is the most reliable predictor of recovery. Valid protects the relationship.

> Most people who say the wrong thing do it because they've never practiced the right one. Valid is the practice.

> Real talk: your instincts are not enough. Practiced responses are. Valid builds them.

---

**Type 05 — The Specific Scenario Headline**
Replaces abstract language with the concrete situation.

> "Nobody would miss me anyway." Three seconds. What do you say?

> Your colleague is speaking too fast to follow and it's 7am before the board presentation. What happens next is about what you've practiced.

> Your father hasn't answered his phone in six days. When he does, Valid is the difference between the right thing and what comes out first.

---

**Type 06 — The Professional Headline**
For clinicians. Evidence-forward. CE-framed.

> Theoretical frameworks collapse under the pressure of actual therapeutic encounters. Valid compresses 40+ hours of supervised exposure into 2–3 hours of structured scenario play.

> You know what validation is. You struggle to produce it when a patient discloses suicidal ideation at minute 48 of a 50-minute session. Valid builds the practiced response that holds.

> Continuing education that rewards clinical judgment, not procedural compliance.

---

### Forbidden Headline Types

**The Inspiration Headline**
> "You have the power to make a difference."
*Brill: conversion rate zero. Feld: belongs on a motivational poster, not a product. Adisa: centers the caregiver's self-image. Okafor: no specific claim.*

**The Question Headline (that implies the answer is obviously yes)**
> "Want to be there for the people you love?"
*Brill: "Everyone wants that. You've differentiated nothing." All four: rejected.*

**The Vague Benefit Headline**
> "Valid: better conversations about mental health."
*Feld: "BetterHelp could run this ad. Headspace could run this ad. Any app with the word 'better' in its name could run this ad."*

**The Awareness Headline**
> "Mental health matters. And Valid helps."
*All four: rejected. Okafor: "What kind of help? This is not a claim." Brill: "This is not a sentence."*

**The Emoji Headline**
> *Any headline containing an emoji.*
*All four: no discussion required.*

### Card Face Copy

**Scenario text — always Cormorant Garamond italic**

Scenario text is not marketing copy. It is the game's voice — a specific person speaking in a specific moment. It follows different rules from all other copy:

- Always first person or direct address from the person in crisis to the player
- Always a complete situation, not a general condition
- Always specific: time of day, relationship, channel (text, call, in person, online)
- Always quoted — the scenario is someone's words, not a summary of their state
- Never uses the word "struggle," "journey," "challenge," or any other softening noun

**Approved scenario copy examples:**

> *"Your best friend texts at midnight: 'I don't see the point in trying anymore.'"*

> *"Your coworker is having a panic attack in the bathroom before the 9am presentation. You can hear it through the door."*

> *"Your teenager keeps asking 'What if I fail?' about every test, every social situation, every decision. It's been three months."*

> *"Your mother calls to say she hasn't left the house since Tuesday. Today is Sunday."*

> *"Someone you follow posts: 'Nobody would miss me anyway.' It's 11pm and you don't know them well."*

**Instruction line — always DM Sans small caps**

> WRITE YOUR FIRST INSTINCT. THEN TURN THIS CARD.

> FORMULATE YOUR CLINICAL RESPONSE. CONSIDER: ASSESSMENT, ALLIANCE, TIME CONSTRAINT.

> WHAT DO YOU SAY NEXT? WRITE IT BEFORE YOU LOOK AT THE OPTIONS.

**Rules:**
- Instruction line is always imperative
- Never more than 12 words
- Never explains why — the game mechanics are explained in the booklet. The card instructs, not teaches.

---

### Phase Prompts — In-Game Copy

**Phase 01 — Diagnostic**

> Before you look at any options: what would you actually say?
> Write it. Exactly. No editing for what sounds right.
> This is diagnostic, not evaluated. Your instinct is the starting point.

**Phase 02 — Analysis**

> Three responses. One works better than the others. The question isn't which — it's why.
> The mechanism matters more than the answer. Understanding why a response fails
> means you'll recognize failure in situations these cards don't cover.

**Phase 03 — Integration**

> How did your instinct compare to what you just read?
> Not whether you were right — whether you can see the difference.
> The gap between those two things is exactly where Valid lives.

### Onboarding Copy

**First time a player opens the box:**

> Before you play, read this.
>
> Mental health awareness is not mental health fluency. Knowing that someone you love has depression is not the same as knowing what to say when they tell you they don't see the point.
>
> Valid closes the gap between those two things — not by teaching you what mental illness is, but by practicing what to do when it shows up in a conversation you didn't expect.
>
> You will write things in this game that surprise you. Some will be more useful than you thought. Some will be further from helpful than felt. Both of those discoveries are the game working.
>
> Professional help — for the person you love and for you — is not a sign of failure. It is the recognition that mental illness requires clinical expertise layered on top of human connection. Valid is the human connection practice. It is not the clinical expertise.
>
> When you're ready: begin with the Social Pathway that feels most relevant to you.

*Okafor note: The last paragraph in the onboarding is a non-negotiable requirement. The distinction between Valid's scope and clinical care must be stated before the game begins, not after.*

---

### Completion / Milestone Copy

**Principle:** Milestone copy is always specific to what the player did, not generic about who they are.

**Never:**
> "You're making a difference. Well done."

**Always specific:**
> "Phase One instinct: Tier 1 response.
> Phase Three instinct: Tier 2 response.
> That shift — that is the game working."

**Never:**
> "You've shown real growth today."

**Always specific:**
> "Three scenarios. Depression pathway. Your response to the midnight text moved from reassurance to acknowledgment. That is a practiced instinct that didn't exist two hours ago."

**Session complete — the closing statement:**

> What just happened in this room is more important than any score.
>
> The next time someone you love reaches out in a moment of real distress, you will have practiced this. The response you reach for will be different.
>
> That difference is not measurable in points. But it is real, and it belongs to you now.

*Adisa's contribution: "The milestone copy is the only place in the game where Valid is allowed to be emotional. Because it has earned it. It has made the player work. The emotion at the end is not performance — it is the actual consequence of the work. But it must be specific. If it generalizes, it cheapens the work."*

*Okafor's requirement: The final sentence of every professional version copy block must distinguish Valid's scope from clinical supervision. This is not a legal hedge — it is a clinical responsibility.*

### Crisis-Adjacent Copy Rules

When Valid's copy is near content related to suicidal ideation, self-harm, or acute psychiatric crisis, five rules apply without exception:

**Rule 01:** Always include a crisis resource. Not at the end of a long paragraph. In its own line.

> If someone in your life is in crisis right now: **988** (US Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) · **Crisis Text Line: text HOME to 741741**

**Rule 02:** Never use suicidal ideation as a hook or teaser. It can appear in scenario cards as a fully-contextualized situation. It cannot appear as a headline, a social post, or an acquisition touchpoint.

**Rule 03:** Never imply that a caregiver who has played Valid is prepared to handle an acute suicidal crisis alone. Always add: "If you believe someone is at immediate risk, call 988 or emergency services."

**Rule 04:** Scenario cards that include suicidal ideation are in the Advanced tier only. The card face copy states: "This is an Advanced scenario. If you have personal experience with suicidal ideation, you may choose to skip this card."

**Rule 05:** Social media content never uses a scenario involving suicidal ideation as a teaser, a hook, or an example. Social copy may reference that the game addresses these scenarios — it does not reproduce the scenarios themselves.

*Okafor: "These are not suggestions. They are the conditions under which I am willing to endorse this product to my patients' families."*

## Part VII — The Copy Audit

### Four Tests Every Piece of Copy Must Pass

Before any Valid copy is approved, it passes four sequential tests — one from each auditor. Failure at any test sends the copy back, not forward.

---

**Test 01 — The Okafor Test**
*"Does this copy make a claim Valid cannot prove — or could it be read by someone in acute distress and cause harm?"*

Apply to:
- Every claim about outcomes ("Valid improves...", "Valid prevents...", "Valid helps people recover...")
- Every piece of copy near crisis-related content
- Every piece of copy that implies a caregiver has acquired clinical capability

Fail condition: A reasonable clinician would object to the claim, or a person in crisis could read the copy and defer professional help as a result.

---

**Test 02 — The Brill Test**
*"Does this copy persuade anyone of anything specific — or is it the brand thinking out loud?"*

Apply to:
- Every headline
- Every first sentence of a body copy block
- Every CTA
- Every piece of copy in a paid acquisition context

Fail condition: The copy could be removed and the argument would not suffer. Or: the copy describes Valid's mission without describing its specific value to a specific person in a specific situation.

---

**Test 03 — The Adisa Test**
*"Is this copy speaking to people with mental illness, or about them — and does it treat the person in crisis as an agent?"*

Apply to:
- Every piece of copy that mentions people with mental illness (not caregivers)
- Every scenario-framing sentence
- Every piece of copy that describes Valid's social value
- Every milestone or completion copy

Fail condition: A person with lived experience of the condition being described would not recognise themselves in the copy, or would feel described rather than addressed.

---

**Test 04 — The Feld Test**
*"Could any mental health brand publish this copy without changing a word?"*

Apply to:
- Every headline
- Every tagline
- Every piece of social copy
- Every brand-level statement (mission, positioning, about pages)

Fail condition: Replace "Valid" with "BetterHelp," "NAMI," "Headspace," or "the Samaritans." If the sentence still works, it fails.

---

### The Audit in Practice — Before and After

**Draft copy:** "Valid helps you show up for the people you love when they need you most."

*Okafor:* No clinical claim made. No harm risk. Pass.
*Brill:* "Show up" is vague. "When they need you most" is vague. Nothing specific. Fail.
*Adisa:* "The people you love" is acceptable. "When they need you most" is soft. Weak pass.
*Feld:* "BetterHelp helps you show up for the people you love when they need you most." Works perfectly. Fail.

**Verdict: Fail (Brill, Feld).** Rewrite required.

**Revised copy:** "When someone you love texts at midnight saying they don't see the point, Valid is the practice you did before that moment."

*Okafor:* Specific scenario. No overclaim. References practice, not capability. Pass.
*Brill:* Specific situation. Specific benefit. Stakes established in 22 words. Pass.
*Adisa:* Names the person's actual words, not a softened description. Centers the person's experience. Pass.
*Feld:* "When someone you love texts at midnight saying they don't see the point, BetterHelp is the practice you did before that moment." Doesn't work — "practice" is Valid's specific mechanism. Pass.

**Verdict: Pass all four.** Approved.

## Part VIII — Writing the Difficult Moments

### How Valid Writes About Mental Illness

Valid writes about mental illness by name. Not "mental health challenges." Not "difficult times." Depression. Anxiety. Bipolar disorder. Psychosis. Suicidal ideation.

The naming is not clinical coldness. It is the opposite: it proves to the reader that Valid has read the material, understands the experience, and is not afraid of the words. People who live with these conditions have spent decades watching their experiences softened into metaphor by people who are uncomfortable with the reality. Valid does not do this.

**Approved naming:**
- A depressive episode
- A panic attack
- Bipolar disorder
- Suicidal ideation
- Psychosis / psychotic episode
- Obsessive thoughts
- Acute anxiety

**Never:**
- Mental health struggles (too soft)
- Emotional difficulties (too soft)
- Going through something hard (unspecific)
- Not in a good place (unspecific, patronising)
- Dark thoughts (metaphorical, imprecise)

---

### How Valid Writes About Suicide and Crisis

Directly. Without sensationalism. With a crisis resource always visible.

The copy uses "suicidal ideation" rather than "dark thoughts," "thoughts of suicide" rather than "thinking about ending it all." Clinical language is not cold here — it is precise, and precision is what makes someone feel that their experience has been understood accurately.

Copy near this content always includes: "If someone you know is in acute crisis, call 988 or emergency services."

Valid does not use this content in acquisition copy. The subject appears in gameplay contexts where the player has entered the game and agreed to engage with this material.

---

### How Valid Writes About Professional Referral

As an upgrade, not a failure.

**Never:**
> "If things get to be too much, please seek professional help."

*Adisa: "This frames professional help as the last resort for people who've exceeded their capacity. It is not. It is the appropriate response to clinical need, regardless of caregiver capacity."*

**Always:**
> "Valid gives you the practiced responses that keep the relationship intact. Professional care gives the person you love the clinical expertise that relationship alone cannot provide. Both are necessary. Neither replaces the other."

---

### How Valid Writes About Imperfect Responses

Valid's educational architecture depends on players writing responses that are less than ideal. The copy that surrounds this must not make players feel judged for their initial instinct — but it must not validate ineffective responses either.

**Phase 01 copy rule:** The prompt never implies that the first instinct is wrong. It establishes the first instinct as information, not performance.

> "Write what you would actually say. Not what you think you should say. Your instinct is the starting point, not the final answer. This phase is diagnostic."

**Phase 03 copy rule:** The reflection prompt acknowledges the gap without shaming it.

> "How did your instinct compare to the response options you saw? The distance between those two things is not a grade. It is the space where learning happened — and happened only because you wrote the first one down."

## Part IX — Anti-Patterns

### Patterns to Kill on Sight

These are recurring failures in mental health brand copy. Any Valid writer who produces them has not read this document.

**Anti-pattern 01 — The Empathy Promise**
> "Valid builds empathy."

Kill it. Empathy is a quality the reader already has. Saying Valid "builds" it implies it was absent. The brand's claim is that Valid builds the *expression* of empathy in specific high-pressure contexts — which is different, specific, and defensible.

---

**Anti-pattern 02 — The Self-Congratulatory Mission**
> "We believe that every person deserves to feel heard, seen, and supported."

Kill it. This is a statement about the brand's values, not a claim about what the brand does. Every human alive believes this. It differentiates nothing.

---

**Anti-pattern 03 — The Passive Caregiver**
> "Valid helps you support your loved one through their mental health journey."

Kill it twice. "Loved one" erases the specific person. "Mental health journey" is the worst phrase in the wellness lexicon. "Support" is unspecific. "Helps you" is passive. Four failures in one sentence.

---

**Anti-pattern 04 — The Overclaim**
> "Valid could save a life."

Kill it immediately. Okafor: "This is the claim that creates the mother who doesn't call 988." The claim Valid can make is: "Valid builds practiced responses that protect relationships that are the most reliable predictors of long-term recovery." That is the accurate version of this claim. It is also more persuasive.

---

**Anti-pattern 05 — The Vague Endorsement**
> "Mental health professionals recommend Valid."

Kill it. Which professionals? What do they recommend it for? What is the basis for the recommendation? This is either a specific, quotable endorsement from a named professional, or it is nothing.

---

**Anti-pattern 06 — The Awareness Pivot**
> "Valid started as a game. But it became a movement."

Kill it. Valid is a game. A precision skill-building system that happens to be a game. The moment it becomes a movement, it has joined the category of things that raise awareness without building capacity. Valid does not want to be a movement. It wants to change what people say in the three seconds after the phone rings.

---

**Anti-pattern 07 — The Gentle Disclaimer**
> "Please remember that Valid is just a game and not a substitute for professional help."

Kill the word "just." Valid is not "just" a game. It is a deliberate application of validated learning mechanisms to a problem that books, workshops, and support groups have failed to solve. The disclaimer is required. The self-diminishment is not.

**Approved form:**
> "Valid builds practiced responses for non-clinical contexts. It does not replace professional mental health care. 988 · Crisis Text Line: HOME to 741741."
